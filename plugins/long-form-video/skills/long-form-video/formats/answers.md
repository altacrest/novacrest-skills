# Format: answers

One video that answers a cluster of real questions people search for, one question per segment. It fits search
demand on YouTube and Google, and every answer becomes a short with the question as its hook.

- **Shape**: a cold open (the questions this video answers, and why they matter), then one to three
  `## PART n: Name` sections grouping 6 to 12 questions, then `## OUTRO`. About 8 to 20 minutes.
- **Segment**: `**N. The question, as people type it?**` (the title must end in "?"), then the answer: the direct
  answer in the first two sentences, then the example, the number or the story that makes it stick. 80 to 150 words,
  and it must make sense on its own, with no "as we said earlier".
- **Looks**: one per answer, two when it runs past about 100 words.
- **Cards**: the part card; each answer opens with the question block on screen. Chapters are the questions.
- **Shorts**: every answer.

## Find the questions

1. In the user's signed-in Chrome, open YouTube Studio for the brand's channel, then Analytics, then the Research
   tab (the page of what people are looking for: the channel's viewers' searches and searches across YouTube). Read
   the searches and the "content gap" topics that touch the niche. Ask the user to sign in if it isn't.
2. Check search demand for the candidate questions with the SE Ranking connector when it's there (keyword questions
   and their volumes), or another keyword source the user has.
3. Pick one topic and 6 to 12 questions inside it that a single video can answer well. Prefer questions the brand's
   buyers ask, and a few that anyone curious would click.
4. Answer each from real sources, one *(sources: ...)* line per answer. No invented numbers.

## Script notes

- Write the question exactly as people search it (that's the chapter and the short's title), and answer it straight away.
- Brand reads go in two or three answers where the brand truly is part of the answer, marked with *(card: ...)*,
  and only from the brand's claims files.
- The title: the main question, or "<Topic>: N Questions Answered".
