# Format: argument

The problem, the possible futures, the verdict, the fix. It states a real operational problem, looks at several ways
the world could go (fuel prices, tariffs, a demand spike, a geopolitical shock), leads the viewer to a reasoned
conclusion, and only then shows the solution. The house rule: at least half the runtime is the problem side.

- **Shape**, in this order:
  - a cold open with the sharpest example of the problem;
  - `## THE PROBLEM: Name`: two to four segments of real operational failures, with sources;
  - `## FUTURE 1: Name`, `## FUTURE 2: Name` (two to four futures): each is a scenario driven by something outside
    the warehouse, and what it would do to the problem;
  - `## THE VERDICT`: the conclusion the futures lead to, in plain words;
  - `## THE FIX: Name`: what to do about it, the brand's part only from its claims files, at most half the body
    (compile.py fails a longer fix);
  - `## OUTRO`.
  About 10 to 25 minutes.
- **Segments**: numbered and titled like the other formats (the title is the lower third). One story can run longer
  than an iceberg entry, so segments are often 120 to 250 words.
- **Looks**: a story needs a new picture every 25 to 50 words, so most segments have two to five looks
  (*(look: ...)* lines, in order; the engine splits the segment at the sentence ends nearest an even share).
  A *(stat: "BIG" "one line")* fact card counts as a look and is good for the numbers in the futures.
- **Cards**: each section's card shows where the viewer is: THE PROBLEM, FUTURES, THE VERDICT, THE FIX. Chapters
  are the sections.
- **Shorts**: segments don't stand alone, so mark 3 to 6 passages that do: `*(short: "first words" "last words")*`
  inside a segment, each 25 to 60 seconds, starting with a line that works as a hook.

## Research

- **The problem**: real, documented operational failures (recalls, stockouts, mis-ships, shrink, port jams), with
  people, numbers and dates, and sources. No made-up scenarios dressed as fact.
- **The futures**: each built on a real, current driver (a published forecast, a policy change, a price series), with
  its source. Say what is forecast and what is fact.
- **The verdict**: it must follow from the futures; a reader should be able to check the reasoning.
- **The fix**: what any business could do, then where the brand helps, from its claims files only.
